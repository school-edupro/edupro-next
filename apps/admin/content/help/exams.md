# Exams and report cards: a guide for the examination cell and teachers

## Who does what

| Person               | Does                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Academic coordinator | Exam types and grade scales, exams and their subjects, each class's subject order and choice groups, entry locks, report-card templates and releases |
| Subject teacher      | Enters marks for their subject and sections (teacher app or admin)                                                                                   |
| Exam office          | Students' subject choices; enters marks and codes for every section (admin), e.g. from paper sheets; cannot reopen locked marks                      |
| Class teacher        | Their section's subject choices; indicators, remarks, exam attendance, height and weight (the exam register)                                         |
| Principal            | Reads results analytics; approves the release                                                                                                        |
| Report cards         | The principal sees every class; a coordinator the classes they coordinate; a class teacher their own section (checks, previews and prints only)      |
| Families             | Read the released card in the parent app and download the PDF (unless withheld for fee default)                                                      |

## Before the exam

1. **Exams → Exams setup**: one menu entry, five tabs. Each thing is set in one place only; Excel and PDF of each list sit beside it.
   - **Types, grade scales, marks codes**: exam types (PT, half-yearly, annual) and the grade scale per class band. **Edit** on a type changes its name, weightage, order or status; the code stays, and an inactive type is not offered for new exams.
   - **Subject marks** (the old Subject Master): for each exam type and class, the subjects its exams hold with their **maximum** and **pass marks**, a graded subject's **scale** (maximum 0 = grade only) and **Optional**. The grid at the top shows, per type and class, how many subjects are set (— = not set); open a cell to set its list (a list not set starts from the subjects the class studies, in its order). **Max / Pass for all** fills every subject with marks; **Copy** sends the list to other classes of the type (only the subjects each class studies) and to other exam types of the class; **Fill from an exam** takes an exam already set up (every class of it); **Copy from last year** brings last year's lists (what is set stays). Excel and PDF of a type. A sub-subject (Geography under Social Science) is a paper of its own, or, ticked **Part of**, a part of its subject: it then has a maximum only (the subject's pass marks, scale and Optional are for the total) and the subject's parts must add up to its maximum (Social Science 35 = Geography 13 + Political Science 22). When the sub-subjects of a subject already add up to it, the list offers **Make them parts of …**. Otherwise a subject's parts come from Class set-up or the exam. Every exam of the type takes its papers and marks from here: a **new exam** (or a class added to it) gets the class's list at once; the exam's **Set-up → Classes and subjects** step shows the list with the marks read-only, and only the ticks (which papers the class sits), the parts (adding up to the maximum) and the progress card change there. A subject with parts in Subject marks is entered in those parts, each by its sub-subject's teacher: the exam shows them, not changed there, and they follow the list until a mark is entered (a part line is not a paper of its own; an exam that holds it as a paper says so). Otherwise a part picked as a sub-subject takes its maximum from that sub-subject's line in Subject marks (else what is left of the subject's maximum). A class with no list yet says so, with **Fill the subject marks from this exam**. When a list changes, every exam of the type follows at once where no mark is entered yet; a paper with marks keeps its own and shows **Differs from the subject marks**, with **Apply the subject marks** (a reason; refused while a mark is above the new maximum, the students are named). A paper the exam holds that is not in the list is marked so: untick it, or add it to the list. A copied exam follows the list of its type.
2. **What each exam takes**: on Exams setup → Types, tick for each type what its exams take
   (marks, indicators / progress card, remarks, attendance, health), e.g. a periodic test marks only.
   A new exam takes its type's; Set-up → Details changes them for one exam. What an exam does not
   take has no tab, no column and no entry (teachers do not see the exam for it).
   **Indicators and HPC → Class mapping**: the set each class is rated on; new exams use it.
3. **Exams → Exams of the session → New exam**, in three steps:
   - **Details**: the type, name, dates and classes (or start from **a copy of an earlier exam**:
     its classes, subjects, marks, parts and progress card sets come with it). Leave the code blank
     and it is made from the type and the year (HY-2026).
   - **Classes and subjects**: one class at a time. The subjects the class studies are ticked for
     you; type the maximum and pass marks once and **Apply** them to every ticked subject, or **Copy
     from** another class. **One figure** on a row splits that subject into parts (Theory and
     Practical, or Physics, Chemistry and Biology entered by their own teachers). Choose the class's
     indicator or progress card set at the foot. **Save and go to** the next class.
   - **Date sheet**: each paper's date and time (**Apply** one time to every paper) and a note
     printed under it (reporting time, what to bring). **Print** gives one class or every class side
     by side.
     Tick **show on the parent portal** so the date sheet appears in the apps. A subject or a class
     with marks entered can't be taken out of the exam (its marks would be lost).
4. **Exams → Subject choices** (once a session):
   - **Class set-up**: **Entered in parts** splits a subject into its sub-subjects (Social Science:
     Geography, History, Pol. Sc.) with a share each; every exam of the class gets these parts, each
     entered by its own teacher. The mark registers show the parts; the report card the total.
   - **Class set-up** is the class's subject template: **Number of subjects** (e.g. 5 for VI and VII, 6
     for VIII), then for Subject-1 … Subject-5 the subject(s) allowed there: one (English: everyone) or
     several (Hindi, Sanskrit, German: the student chooses one; name the choice "Second language").
     Every student fills every slot; the students' grid shows these slots in this order.
   - **Class set-up** also sets the order the class's subjects print in on report cards and the mark register
     (↑ ↓; a choice group moves with its subjects), who takes each subject (everyone, one of a group,
     or those who pick it), the choice groups ("Second language: pick 1", or "Additional: pick 0 or
     1", kept out of the total) and, for XI–XII, the streams with their subjects.
   - **Students**: one row per student, laid out like the old Student Subject Mapping: the stream, then
     Subject-1, Subject-2, … in the class's order, each with its **Optional** tick (the student's own
     additional subject, out of the total); **Bulk assign all** on a column sets it for everyone; the
     bin clears a student back to "not set". Press **Save the choices** to keep the changes.
     **Set many at once** fills a column for those without one; **Missing a choice** lists who is
     left; **Bring last year's choices** fills class XII from XI. The class teacher can do the same
     for their section in the teacher app. A subject with marks stays (mark it NA first).
   - **Opt** on a row: the student's own additional subject (kept out of the total, printed last).
     **Clear** puts a student back to "not set"; **History** shows who changed what. **Find a
     student in any section** takes an admission no.
   - **A subject changed during the year** (Hindi in PT1, German from PT2): **Change subject** on the
     student's row (the office), choose the new subject, the first exam with it and the reason. PT1
     keeps Hindi with its marks; from PT2 the German teacher enters the marks; the term result
     carries PT1's Hindi marks into German's line, noted "PT1 in Hindi". **Undo** while German has no
     marks.
   - **Whole class**: every section's "taking / students" for each subject, who is not set or
     missing a choice, and **Set for the whole class** (one column for every section at once).
     PDF and Excel of the summary and of every student.
5. Optional: indicator sets, the remark bank. **Exams → Exams setup → Indicators and HPC** holds the sets in up to
   three levels (area → item → sub-item); **Add the progress card set** brings the NCERT
   foundational-stage Holistic Progress Card for Balvatika to Class II (domains, curricular goals,
   competencies; Beginner / Progressing / Proficient printed with Stream / Mountain / Sky) and **Add
   the progress card template** its card. Choose a class's set on the exam's page. A top item may
   have **own levels** (Always, Mostly, Sometimes, Rarely for observations, learning habits, peer and
   parent sections): its items are rated on those, and the card prints a key line for them.
   **Exams setup → Areas and level scales** holds the lists the set editor picks from: the areas
   (in the order the card prints them and teachers see them; a new name renames the area in every
   set) and the level scales (changing a scale's levels changes them in every set using it; to
   rename a level write its new words in its place, and a level with saved entries stays).
   An item placed under a heading is in the heading's area.

## Following an exam

- The exam list shows each exam's **stage** (Set-up → Marks entry → Verification → Results → Cards
  released), worked out from what is done, how many marks are entered and the **next step** button.
  Filter by type, class or stage.
- An exam opens on its **Overview**: the next step, the figures, and class by class how many marks
  are entered and verified, indicators, remarks, attendance, health and results. Every page of the
  exam has the same tabs: Overview · Set-up · Marks · Indicators · Remarks · Attendance.
- **Lock the whole exam** (at the top) and the **Entry locks** on the Overview (marks, indicators,
  remarks, attendance or health of a class) are the **school admin's** alone: coordinators and the exam
  office enter, submit, verify and send back as before, but do not lock or open. While the exam is
  locked no one enters or changes marks. **Unlocking the whole exam** also reopens every submitted and
  verified subject sheet (the dialog says how many and asks the reason, kept in each sheet's history),
  so every teacher can enter again and submit again for verification; a class whose results are
  published on a report card stays as it is (revoke the class first). A single sheet can still be
  reopened from its tile in Marks status. After locking the whole exam, opening one class (its Marks
  lock on the Overview) lets that class's teachers enter while the other classes stay locked; the
  button at the top then reads **Lock the whole exam** again (the exam is no longer wholly locked).

## Marks entry

- Teachers enter marks in the teacher app **Marks** (or **Exams → open exam → Marks → Enter
  marks**): choose the section and the subject (it opens at once; **Previous / Next** go through every
  section's subjects), type a mark or pick a code; Enter goes to the next student and a mark above the
  maximum shows in red. **Save**, or **Save and submit** once every student who takes the subject has
  a mark or a code. The page asks before unsaved marks are left.
- The coordinator **locks** a subject when entry is complete; a reopen needs a reason and is audited.
- **Who can type marks**: subject and class teachers, and the **Exam office** role (Access → give it to
  an office login). The school admin and the coordinator verify and reopen, so their marks pages
  show the marks as text: entering and reopening are never the same person.
- The exam register belongs to the class teacher: in the admin, **Remarks** (the general remark and
  the categories, saved together), **Attendance → Exam attendance** (the % shows as you type) and
  **Attendance → Health** (height, weight and blood group; the BMI shows as you type). In the teacher
  app the class teacher has **Height and weight** (the exams that take it, each pupil's last reading,
  BMI worked out on saving; one record per pupil per exam) and **Exam attendance** as their own items,
  each with **By Excel** (download the sheet, fill, upload, check, save).
- **Who enters what** (indicators and remarks): the class teacher enters every indicator and every
  remark category of the section. A teacher given an **Indicator** assignment (Academics → Teacher
  assignments) rates only its areas (or the single indicators it names) in that section; the class
  teacher's page says who else enters an area. A subject category (e.g. Mathematics) is also its
  subject teacher's; a **Remarks** assignment gives categories to any teacher. A teacher with nothing
  to write is told why ("No remark category is set for Class VI…"). The teacher app shows a teacher's
  indicator areas as tabs, each with what is entered ("Marathi (0/40)"). Both pages have **By Excel** with
  only that teacher's columns. The teacher app's home lists **To enter**: per open exam, what is theirs
  and not done (marks not submitted, indicators, remark categories, height and weight).
- The progress card: in the teacher app **Indicators and progress card** (or **Exams → open exam →
  Indicators**) the class teacher moves between the areas (tabs; nothing is lost) and gives each
  child a level per competency, saving every area at once
  (or a written note), then per child writes "All about me", the parent's feedback (from paper) and
  their own observations, and adds portfolio photos (up to twelve; the card prints six). Parents may
  write their feedback in the parent app under **Results**; it is kept as they wrote it. The
  indicators lock of the class closes all of it.

**Marks status** (Exam → Marks → Marks status) is a grid per class: a row per section, a column per subject.
Each tile shows the marks entered out of the students who take the subject and its colour says where it
stands (not started, in progress, entered, submitted, verified; "—" when nobody takes it). Under the
count is the section's **subject teacher** (Academics → Teacher assignments), or "No teacher mapped".
Pick a tile to see who submitted or verified it and to submit, verify, send back or reopen it.

## Results and report cards

- **Work out results** is on the exam's **Overview** (with when they were last worked out): the
  totals, %, grade and rank of each pupil, used by the exam report cards and the dashboards. When it is
  the next step, the Overview's next step is this button.
- An exam has no Results or Reports tab: the analyses, the register, toppers, teacher-wise, missing
  marks, absentees and attendance are a **mark register**'s Run reports (Exams → Registers). The old
  pages (Analysis, Mark register, Promotion, Reports) still open from an old link.
- **Date sheet in the apps**: families see their child's papers under **Exam date sheet** in the
  parent app once the exam is shown on the parent portal; teachers see their classes' date sheets in
  the teacher app (**Exam date sheet**).
- Every page of an exam is in English and Hindi (the language switch at the top).
- **Teacher app pages from the admin app**: the home page's **Quick actions** has a **Teacher app**
  tab with **Marks entry**, **Indicators and progress card**, **Remarks** and **Exam date sheet** (each
  only for people who may use it). A tile opens that page of the teacher app in a new tab.

1. **Exams → open exam → Results → Compute**: totals, percentages, grades, ranks per section and class.
2. **Exams → Report cards → Templates**: the layout per band (HPC for primary, CCE for middle,
   components for secondary, theory/practical for senior); preview with a real pupil.
3. **Exams → Report cards → + New release**: first the **kind of card**, then a code, a name and the
   exams the cards carry; tick **Withhold the card while a fee is due** with the amount if the school
   withholds cards.
   - **Exam card** (a periodic test such as PT1 or PT2): tick the test; the code and name come from it.
     It prints the **Periodic test slip** of each band (maximum marks, marks obtained and grade per
     subject, then the total, percentage and grade; no rank, no pass or fail). No term result is needed.
     The VI–VIII slip is one A4 page of ruled boxes, as the Term 1 card below.
   - **Term card** (T1, T2): every exam of the term; prints the term result of the locked mark register
     with that term code (for example the VI-VIII card: Periodic test, Subject enrichment and the term
     exam, the marks obtained and the grade).
   - **Annual card**: every exam of the year.
     A class can have **several cards live at once**: for Class VI make an exam card PT1, an exam card PT2
     and a term card T1, and publish each for Class VI. Families see every published card in the parent
     app, newest first, each marked Exam card, Term card or Annual card. **Report cards → By class** shows
     one class's cards side by side, with whether each is published, the PDFs made and the papers not
     verified, and **Publish** / **Revoke** for that class.
     **Whose classes**: the principal (and the school and group admins) sees every class. A coordinator
     sees the classes they coordinate and a class teacher their own section, as set in **Academics →
     Teacher assignments**; the page says "You see only your own classes: …". A class teacher checks the
     readiness, previews and prints their section's cards; holds, set-up and new cards are the
     coordinator's and the principal's, and only the principal publishes. Subject teachers do not open
     Report cards.
     The release opens in its workspace, with four steps:
   - **Set-up**: the name, the exams and the card design of each class (a class's own design comes
     before its band's: Class IX and X can print different cards).
     **Card designs** (Report cards → Card designs) has the ready-made designs: **Install the
     ready-made cards** (Learning record I–II and III–V, Report card VI–VIII, Report card VI–VIII
     (Term 1), IX, X, XI, XII) and
     **Install the learning record (Balvatika–II)**. **Report card VI–VIII (Term 1)** prints one A4
     page of ruled boxes: the school with a logo each side (the second one is the design's **Second
     emblem** picture), the pupil, the term's parts with the marks obtained and the grade, the
     co-scholastic grades beside height, weight and attendance, discipline, the class teacher's
     remark, place and date, the parent's, class teacher's, coordinator's, vice principal's and
     principal's signatures and the seal (their images from Signatures), and the grading key. The learning record prints on A4 like the school's
     old card: the cover ("My name is …", the photo), the subjects' skills with a Term I and a Term II
     grade and each subject's remarks beside them, the co-scholastic areas, health / attendance / "I
     participated in" / "My teachers say" / signatures, and the evaluation key. The terms are the year's
     exams that use the card's indicator set, by date: a Term I card leaves Term II blank. Installing it
     also adds the indicator set **Learning record (I–II)** (A* to D), its key and English, Mathematics,
     Hindi and EVS remark categories; give the set to the Class I–II exams (Exams → Set-up), or keep the
     NCERT set, which prints on the same pages. In the **Designer** of a learning record, **Pictures**
     takes the school's own pictures (cover, a second emblem, one per page; PNG or JPEG up to 1 MB).
   - **Readiness**: class by class, a checklist of what is done (✓) and what is still needed (!): the
     card design, the term result worked out from a locked mark register, each exam's papers verified,
     remarks and attendance. Each line links to the page that fixes it. The principal can publish
     anyway.
   - **Cards** (like the old Exam Report Card): one line per card design (for example "Class VI–VIII").
     On a line pick the **Class** (a section, or "VI (all sections)"), the **Exam Type** (this release's
     term, already picked) and the students: **Select Roll No.** takes a range in roll-number order in
     each section ("11–20" = the 11th to 20th student of each section, not roll numbers 11 to 20;
     **Whole class** = everyone), **Status** picks active, inactive (left, transferred) or all, and
     **Search Admission** makes one student's card instead. **Generate Report Card** makes the PDF in
     the background (the button counts the seconds) and downloads it when ready; the **eye** shows the
     cards at once in a side panel with **Previous** / **Next**, **Print**, **Open in a new tab** and
     **Hold** (with a reason; **Show again** undoes it). **Download Log** lists every PDF made for the
     release, with who made it and when; **Settings** opens the Set-up step and **User Guide** this page.
     The PDFs are made by the workers: if a PDF waits, it appears in the Download Log once ready.
   - **Publish**: the office **Sends to the principal**. The principal publishes **class by class**
     (**Publish VI**) or **Publish every class**; each class's families get a message and see the cards
     in the parent app (withheld and held cards stay hidden). **Revoke VI** (with a reason) hides that
     class's cards again and lets its marks be reopened; revoking the last class withdraws the release.
     The table shows, per class, whether it is ready, how many cards are held (**Held**: a click lists
     them), when it was published and how many messages went.
   - **Held cards** (under the class table): the cards held back from the parent and student portal,
     with filters **Class**, **Section** and **Search** (name, admission no or the reason's words), who
     held each and when; **Excel** and **PDF** of the list as filtered. **Show again** (one, or tick
     several) opens the card; where its class is published the family gets the result message at once.
     Cards the fee rule withholds are only counted here ("Withheld for fees"): they open once paid.
     **Hold cards…** opens a side window (the old Report Card Hide page): one **Reason** for the students
     held together (kept for the school; the family sees only "held, contact the office"), and **Also
     hold in** the year's other published cards when wanted. Then either **Pick students** — Class →
     Section → a tick per student (tick-all at the top; a held student shows its reason and **Show
     again**) → **Hold n cards** — or **Upload Excel** — **Download the format**, write an admission no
     per row (and a reason when it differs from the one typed), upload: every row is checked first (not
     found, not in this card's classes, not in your classes, held already, twice in the sheet, no reason)
     and **Hold n cards** holds the rows without a problem. A hold covers this card only. The principal
     and the school admin hold in every class, a coordinator in their classes; a class teacher sees their
     section's held cards, read only.
4. Families see their child's published cards in the parent app (**Results**): **View report card**
   opens the card at once (to read, print or save), and **Report card PDF** also makes the PDF in the
   background (the page shows it by itself when it is ready; the workers make it; asking again while it
   is being made does not queue another). If the PDF takes longer than about 45 seconds the page offers
   **Print / Save as PDF**, which opens the card with the browser's print dialog (Save as PDF makes the
   file at once); the card page has the same button. A card held by the school, or withheld while a fee
   is due, says so instead of the buttons.
5. **Insights → Results analytics**: pass % and mean by class and section, weakest subjects.
6. **Exams → open exam → Reports**: the section-wise subject analysis, mark ranges (the class's grade
   scale, or your own ranges), toppers (top N in the class or section, at or above / below a %, or a
   subject's), the subject teachers' analysis, the upload status and the missing marks; each with
   Excel and PDF. A locked result register's page has **Merit by division**. The report builder has the
   exam marks, exam results and term results datasets for your own reports.
7. **Promotion proposals** (a locked result register of the final term): passed → promote to the next
   class, compartment → held for review (the card says "subject to compartment"), failed → retain,
   absent or incomplete → review, the last class → passed out. Choose next year's section, tick and
   send: the decisions go to **People → Promotions** (applied there) and the cards' "Promoted to" is
   filled.

## Mark registers

**Exams → Registers → Mark Register** (the school's Term Mark Register) sets up once how a class's marks are worked
out, then runs it for any section; the marks always come from the exam entry.

1. **+ New mark register**: six steps with a preview on two sample students (type over a mark or a code
   such as AB to try the formulas).
   - **Details**: the name (unique in the year), the classes, student order (roll, A–Z, rank), layout
     (subjects across the top, or Subject-1, Subject-2 … for different electives) and type (general,
     or exam-wise: pick the exam at Run; the sheet then shows that exam's marks only, with its total,
     %, grade and rank). **The term result** makes the term results for report cards
     and promotion.
   - **Subjects**: tick them; **+ Elective slot** (or **Use the classes' choice groups**) makes one
     block of several subjects, each student with their own. Only a subject with a paper of its own
     can be ticked: a subject's parts are named on its tile (Social Science — parts: Geography, History;
     show them with **Each part a column** in step 3), and a part that is not a paper (Geography marked
     inside Social Science) or a subject with no paper in the year's exams is listed greyed under **Not
     a subject of their own**, with the reason. A part that is also a paper in some exam says which
     ("own paper: ACT2 (VIII); else inside Social Science").
     A ticked subject entered in parts is listed under **Composite subjects** with its sub-subjects
     and whether they show (**Show its parts in each exam** when its columns read only the whole
     marks), and **Child subjects in columns** (a column each, under the exam's name in the heading;
     off: in one cell with their sum). In
     **Subject order** it is marked _composite_ and its sub-subjects are listed under it with their
     own ▲ ▼: their order is the order of their columns in every exam, whatever the subject order.
   - **Columns**, per subject: pick the exam, then **Same marks as entry** (or its parts; a
     composite subject starts on its parts in one cell with their sum), **Average /
     best of / sum**, **Conversion** (scale to a maximum, or a weightage %; "the rest" of a total out
     of) or **Attendance** slabs. Per column: in the total, a grade, round off (.5 kept), hide (kept in
     the maths), show code, and **sub from** (where an ML pro-rata takes its marks). **Copy to
     subjects** and the **Weightage builder** save a long set-up. Copy to subjects says per subject
     what it leaves out (an exam the subject has no paper in, and an average left with nothing to
     average); a subject with none of the exams is greyed ("no paper in PT1-2026") and not copied to.
     A subject entered in parts (Social Science: Geography, History) shows them with **Show its parts
     in each exam** (each exam's parts in one cell with their sum), or a column each with **Parts in
     columns**; Copy to subjects does it for such a subject when its tick is on. Each class shows its
     own parts (History in VI, Political Science in VII), and a class that enters the subject as
     whole marks (Science without parts in VI) shows them in its place. An **elective slot** takes
     the columns its subjects had.
   - **Saving** lists everything to fix. A subject with no paper of its own in the register's exams
     comes first, with the cause ("Geography has no paper of its own in this register's exams: it is
     marked inside Social Science") and **Take them out**, which removes such subjects; save again.
   - **Mark codes**, per exam: count 0, leave out of the maximum, fixed or % marks, pro-rata; holds the
     total ("–"); show the value with "*".
   - **Totals** (round off, grade, % per subject) and **Final** (total, %, grade, rank, result, pass %,
     compartment, theory and practical each, optional subjects, student columns, decimals, class
     average; grading scale and divisions).
2. **Run**: Class (a section, or a class's **all sections (merit)**, by rank or division-wise), Exam,
   Subject; the note above the sheet says what to check (withheld finals, marks not entered).
   **Download**: PDF (paper, way round, fit to width) or Excel, this section or every section.
   **Reports ▾** (each downloads the same way; a count opens the students it stands for):
   - **Result summary** (as the old register prints it): the rules line, then total, passed, failed
     in 1, 2 … subjects (compartment or fail), absent, medical leave, not entered, each with its % of
     those who appeared, and the pass %; then a table per group: student, admission no, the subjects
     with their %, the final.
   - **Analysis** (as the old Result Analysis prints it): the subjects across the top (each language of a
     slot apart) and down the side the total students, absent, appeared, the students in each % band,
     the cumulative counts (bands set in step 6), the highest, lowest and average marks and the passing
     %; a count opens its students. A section: its one table. A class's all sections: the class as one,
     then each section in the same layout. A class's all sections with one **Subject** picked: a row per
     section with its teachers of the subject (each language of a slot apart), then **All sections**.
   - **Entry status** (as the old register prints it): a row per subject with its teachers and
     students, a column per exam — entered of how many with Complete / Partial, "▶ 1 pending" opens the
     names, "–" where the subject has no such paper — and the % of all subjects at the foot; the class
     teacher in the line above. A class's all sections: that table for each section in turn (the same
     exams, so they line up; "pending" names the section), then a grid of the % entered per section and
     subject.
   - **Toppers** (as the old register prints them): "Overall Toppers — Top N · by Final Total" with
     position, final total, % and grade (every tie at the last position shown), each section's top N in
     a class, then "Subject Toppers" — every subject's highest with all ties, "Full marks" marked.
     **Top** takes any number up to 100 (empty = everyone in the % range); **% from – to** keeps only
     the students whose % is in the range before the top N is taken (the final % with All subjects,
     the subject's %); **Order: Lowest first** gives the same lists from the bottom (every final
     counted, not only a pass). With a **Subject** picked the report is that subject's own toppers, by
     its %: the class, then each section (each language of a slot apart), of everyone with marks in
     it. Press **Show** after typing the numbers; the downloads follow the filters.
   - **Missing marks**: the papers with nothing entered (no mark and no code; a code such as AB or ML
     counts as entered). First each section (and the class) against the exams — "3 papers · 2
     students", a click for the names and what is missing — then a table per section: a line per
     subject and exam with anything missing, its teacher (or "no teacher mapped"), missing / takers and
     the students by roll (a subject in parts names the parts missing). **Exam** picks one exam or All
     exams; a **Subject** picked lists that subject only.
   - **Absent students**: AB on a paper, or a part marked absent (Geography in Social Science); ML and
     the other codes are not absences. First each section against the exams (students absent, a click
     for the names), then a table per section: a line per absent student with the parents' mobiles, the
     class teacher, where absent ("MT1: MATHEMATICS · TERM: SOCIAL SCIENCE (Geography)") and how many
     papers. **Exam** and **Subject** as for Missing marks.
   - **Attendance**: the day attendance between **From** and **To** (by default the session's first day
     and today; press **Show** after changing them), a table per section as the old Attendance Report:
     SRN, Class, Roll No, Student ID, Name, **A** (absent), **P** (present — late, short leave, half
     day, on duty and stay back count as present), **X** (on leave), **Total** = A + P + X and **%** =
     P × 100 ÷ (Total − X), so leave does not lower it. Every pupil is listed; one with nothing marked
     shows 0s and no %. The downloads carry the dates.
   - **Teacher-wise**: a block per subject teacher (Academics → Teacher assignments) with the Result
     Analysis figures down the side — Total students, Absent, Appeared, the % bands, the cumulative
     counts (>=50% …), Highest, Lowest and Average marks, Passing % — then Average % and Failed; a
     column per class the teacher teaches ("VI-A · ENGLISH", "(shared)" when two teachers share it) and
     a **Total** of all of them (its Highest, Lowest and Average marks only when the classes have the
     same maximum). A count opens its students; "No teacher mapped" last.
   - **At-risk**: failing (high), compartment (medium), borderline subjects or absent (watch), with a
     margin of 5 or 10 over the pass %; counts by subject.
   - **Failing students**: the students with a final worked out (Compartment / Fail). **Failed in from
     – to** picks by the number of subjects failed (2 to 2 = exactly two, 3 to 4, 2 to empty = two and
     more) and **Final % from – to** by the final; a **Subject** picked keeps those failing it. First
     the counts — sections and the class against Failed in 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 5+ (the range shaded) — and
     each subject's failures by section, then a table per number failed ("Failed in 2 subjects"): a line
     per student with the parents' mobiles (F / M), the class teacher, every subject's marks (the failed
     ones in red), the final, the result and the teachers of the failed subjects. Press **Show** after
     typing the numbers; the downloads follow the filters. Students whose marks are still missing are
     not listed (the note above names them).
   - **Compare sections** (a class's all sections, as the old register prints it): "Overall result" —
     for each section and the class: total students, absent, appeared, the students in each % band and
     the cumulative counts (on the final %), the highest, lowest and average marks (the final total),
     average final %, pass %, compartment and fail (the best section ▲; a count opens its students) —
     then each subject's average %, pass % and highest by section.
3. **Lock** pins a register: saving it then makes its next version (a draft); **Versions** restores an
   old one as a new register; **Delete** sends a draft to the bin (**Deleted** brings it back);
   **Copy** makes one for other classes.
4. **Assign** (managers, or someone given Assign) gives a teacher Run / Edit / Delete on one register;
   the **Rights report** shows who holds what.

### Grade registers (indicators)

**Exams → Registers → Grade Register** keeps the school's grade registers, as the old Grade Register
did: a register says which assessments and indicators a class's grade sheet shows, in which order and
with which headers.

1. **+ New grade register** (register managers: exam office, school admin) opens four steps:
   **Details** (a name unique in the year, the classes — "no grades yet" marks a class with no grades —
   and the student order), **Assessments** (the exams of the year that rate indicators for those
   classes; tick them in print order and give each a short header such as T1), **Indicators** (the
   areas and indicators of the classes' sets: untick what the register leaves out, move with ▲▼, give
   a long indicator a short header) and **Display & Save** (the header as full text or numbered D1, D2
   … with a legend, proper-case names, the text for a blank grade, the **Overall** per area — most
   frequent grade, or by the points of the grade scale, with the scale best first — and the student
   columns). A preview on three sample students shows the sheet as you go.
2. The list has **Edit** (a locked register saves as its next version), **Versions** (restoring a
   version makes a new register), **Copy** (for other classes; the people given rights are not copied),
   **Assign** (Run / Edit / Delete / Assign per person, as for mark registers), **Access** (**All**: who
   may run it sees every indicator; **As per mapping**: a teacher sees only the indicator areas of their
   Indicator assignment), **Lock** / **Unlock**, **Delete** (to the bin; **Deleted** brings it back) and
   the **Rights report**.
3. **Run** (a new tab) shows a **Section** of the register's classes: the **Class sheet** (a row per
   student; area, indicator and term headers; the Overall per area tinted green) or **Student-wise** (a
   table per student). **Filter** by area and indicator. **Grade summary** counts each grade, the blanks
   and the students per indicator and Overall (click a count to see its students); **Analysis** gives
   the % of each grade per area, indicator and Overall for the section, or **Analysis (all sections)**
   side by side. **Download** gives the PDF (paper, way round, fit to width; student-wise a page per
   student) or Excel of what is shown, or of every section.
4. **As per mapping**: in **Academics → Teacher assignments**, an **Indicator** assignment's row shows
   its indicator areas ("All indicator areas" until some are ticked): open it to tick the areas that
   teacher rates in the section (and, optionally, single indicators of them). A register set to As per mapping shows such a teacher only those areas
   (no Indicator assignment of the section: nothing, with a note). Register managers see every area.

### Remark, Attendance and Height & Weight registers

**Exams → Registers** opens with a switch at the top, as in the old Registers menu: **Mark Register**
(the saved registers above), **Remark Register**, **Attendance Register** and **Height & Weight
Register**. The last three are reports of what was entered; nothing is saved.

1. Pick the **Class**, the **Section** (or **All sections**) and tick one or more **Exams** (only the
   exams that have entries for the class are offered), then **Show**.
2. **Remark Register**: per exam the general remark, each remark category used and participation, a
   column each; **Promoted to** when it was given. Long remarks wrap in their cell.
3. **Attendance Register**: per exam days present, days and %; with two or more exams an **Overall**
   (all days present ÷ all days). Below the **Cut-off %** (default 75) is shown in red; each section has
   a **Summary** (students, with attendance, class average %, how many below) and the list of those
   below, lowest first.
4. **Height & Weight Register**: per exam height, weight and **BMI** (Show / Hide); with two or more
   exams the **Change** from the first measurement to the last; **Class averages** per exam. A student
   measured twice in one exam shows the later measurement. It needs the health view right.
5. **Download**: PDF (paper, way round, fit to width) or Excel of what is shown, or of all sections (a
   section a page).

The office (whoever manages or assigns mark registers) sees every section; a teacher sees the
sections they teach this year (class teacher, subject teacher, coordinator: every section of the
class), as set in **Academics → Teacher assignments**. Names typed in capitals print in proper case.

## Remarks by category

**Exams → Exams setup → Remark categories and bank**: name the categories (the old A / B / C / D lists and "any other"),
tie one to a subject (its subject teacher writes it too), to some classes, and choose free text or the
listed remarks only; put remarks in the bank under each category. Teachers write them in the teacher
app **Remarks** (or **Exams → open exam → Remarks**): one per student and category. The remarks lock
of the class closes them; the report card prints them under the remarks.

## Five common questions

1. _A teacher cannot see a section._ They need a teacher assignment for that section and subject this
   year (**Academics → Teacher assignments**).
2. _The card shows the wrong grade._ Check the grade scale assigned to the class for this exam;
   recompute results after fixing it.
3. _A family cannot download the card._ Either the release is not yet released, or the card is
   withheld for a fee balance above the release's minimum.
4. _We need to change a mark after locking._ Reopen the subject (coordinator), correct, lock again;
   recompute results and re-render the card.
5. _Board results._ **Exams → Board results**: upload the CBSE file; pupils match by board roll number
   (kept on the enrolment) or by name; the analysis and the export are available at once.
